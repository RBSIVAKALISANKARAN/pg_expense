PostgreSQL data model

How the database is connected

A human-readable reference for the finance schema: what each table stores, how foreign keys connect the tables, and why each relationship exists.

Section 1

How the tables connect

Account → Allocation

One Account owns two allocation rows: spendable and savings. Allocation.balance is the split of Account.total_balance.

Account → Transaction

Every deposit, expense, allocation or transfer belongs to an Account. This is the wallet ledger relationship.

Account → MoneyLocation

Each wallet Account points to one physical/logical MoneyLocation such as rbsankaran_acc, Amma Cash, Appa Cash, Change Cash or Travel Card.

Owner + MoneyLocation + Account + Allocation → MoneyPool

MoneyPool is the reconciliation layer. It tracks how much money a particular owner has in a particular location and allocation.

Category → SubCategory → Item

This is the expense taxonomy. A Category contains many SubCategories and Items; an Item may optionally belong to a SubCategory.

Transaction → Category / SubCategory / Item

An expense can point into the taxonomy so reports can group spending at category, subcategory or item level. These foreign keys are nullable so uncategorized/custom expenses remain possible.

Item → FoodProfile

A food Item may have one FoodProfile. FoodProfile classifies the item as Food or Drink and stores its food group, health classification and sugary status.

Transaction → FoodEvent → FoodEventItem

Food transactions can have a meal context and one or more food entries. FoodEvent is one-to-one with Transaction; FoodEventItem is many-to-one with FoodEvent.

Transaction → Transaction

related_tx links the two sides of a wallet transfer so outgoing and incoming ledger rows can be reverted together.

SavedQuery / QueryExecutionLog

SavedQuery stores reusable SQL. QueryExecutionLog stores read-only playground execution history, status, duration and errors.

Section 3

Money-flow logic

  1. Deposit: increases Account.total_balance, the selected Allocation.balance and the matching MoneyPool.current_amount.
  2. Expense: decreases those three values together and creates one ledger Transaction.
  3. Wallet transfer: creates an outgoing transaction on the source wallet and an incoming transaction on the destination wallet, linked through related_tx.
  4. Reconciliation: Account.total_balance must equal the sum of its allocations and the sum of its money pools; failed reconciliation aborts the transaction.
  5. Expense taxonomy: Category is the broad bucket, SubCategory refines it, Item identifies the fixed master item, while Transaction.variant remains deliberately free text for unpredictable details such as a brand, provider or food variant.
  6. Food taxonomy: FoodProfile extends a food Item with Food/Drink type, food group, health classification and sugary status. These attributes can be queried through the Item → FoodProfile relationship.
